## Entry point

**Signal type:** New message / transcript received

"User interview transcript," not just "transcript." The Raw Signals doc mixes five different artifact types in one place: a transcript, a support ticket, an executive email, CRM call notes, and a Slack thread. If the trigger just said "new transcript received," it would still fire correctly here, but it wouldn't rule out, say, CRM call notes getting misclassified as a transcript by whatever system tags these. Naming the artifact type keeps the trigger scoped to exactly what we built the tool selection around.
"Raw Signals intake," not a specific tool name. I don't know whether this actually lives in a research repo, a shared doc, an interview tool, or a support/feedback inbox, so I used the name the source document itself uses rather than guessing a system. Swap in the real system name once you know it (e.g., "added to the Dovetail project" or "logged in the #customer-signals channel").

## The flow

1. 1. Retrieval across six sources. Juno pulls Sarah's transcript, dev_dave's Slack thread, the Strategy One-Pager's P0–P3 framework, the Capacity Note's Platform & Infra paragraph, the two roadmap lines, and the Initech pipeline entry, tagging each with a freshness status (fresh/stale/cached/unreachable) as it goes. This is the seven read verbs from the tools section running in sequence, not in parallel, since later steps depend on all six being in hand.

2. Cross-reference against strategy, capacity, and roadmap. The failure described in Sarah's transcript (CSV export crashes, no error message, PDF works fine) gets checked against three things at once: does it match a P0 category in the strategy framework (a reliability failure, yes); does the Capacity Note's owning pod have room to work on it (no, Platform & Infra is fully consumed); and does the roadmap already have committed or planned work that touches this (yes, phase 1 migration and Q4 sharding). This is where the Slack thread earns its place, it's checked for whether it actually corroborates Sarah's crash (same service, plausible size range) rather than just being present.

3. Priority and confidence scoring. Two separate outputs come out of step 2: a priority tier (P0, per the strategy framework) and a confidence score, which is not the same thing. Confidence gets pulled down by anything step 2 couldn't confirm cleanly, most notably that Sarah never states a file size, so the Slack thread's "over 50MB" claim is plausible corroboration, not proof.

4. Pre-ship gate check. Before anything is shown as a finished draft, it runs through the checks from the verification section: every claim traces to one of the six sources, no excluded topic (Pearson, Gavin's email, Acme SSO) leaked in, the roadmap disposition matches the roadmap's actual wording, and the confidence score decides whether it ships as a normal result or a flagged one. This step is what turns "Juno is reviewing this signal…" into either a real draft or an honestly-labeled failure state, per the failure-behavior section.
2. "Reading Sarah's transcript…" → "Checking the Slack thread for corroboration…" → "Checking against the Strategy One-Pager…" → "Checking Platform & Infra capacity…" → "Cross-referencing the Roadmap…" → "Checking revenue impact…" → "Scoring priority and confidence…" → "Running final checks before showing this…"
3. Skip. This is single-path.

## AI moments

**Placement:** Hybrid (multiple placements)

A P0–P3 priority chip with owner and confidence on the transcript row in Raw Signals, expanding to a cited triage card on the Full-Page Canvas: priority tier, recommended owner, roadmap disposition, confidence score with stated reasons, a per-source freshness strip, and an approve/edit/reject decision field.

Contextual surface (on the transcript itself)
A priority chip: "P0" in the strategy framework's color, sitting where the "Juno is reviewing…" badge used to be.
A one-line owner tag: "Platform & Infra" beneath the chip.
A confidence dot: green/yellow/red, matching the 60% threshold from the verification section, no number shown here, just enough to tell the reviewer at a glance whether this needs extra scrutiny before they click in.
Full-Page Canvas (on click)
The priority card itself, structured, not prose: "Priority: P0" with a one-line citation directly beneath it, "Strategy One-Pager, Trust at Scale / reliability incidents."
The owner recommendation: "Platform & Infra" with its own citation, "Engineering Capacity Note: pod fully consumed by legacy reporting API; sharding required before new load."
The roadmap disposition: "Reporting export migration, phase 1 (committed, end of Q3). Does not yet unblock new load." Shown verbatim from the roadmap, not paraphrased, per the pre-ship gate's exact-wording check. If a mismatch was ever caught, this is where the side-by-side comparison would render, Juno's draft language on the left, the roadmap's actual line on the right.
A source freshness strip: six small tiles, one per source, each labeled fresh/stale/cached/unreachable with an age ("Capacity Note: 3 days old"). This is the visual form of the freshness header required in the freshness section.
A confidence panel: the score plus its stated reasons in plain language, "Corroborated by a Slack thread describing similar errors, but Sarah does not state a file size, so the match is not confirmed."
The revenue-impact line: "Initech expansion ($120k ARR) contingent on reporting reliability; eval team has asked about export limits," cited to the Pipeline Snapshot.
The original transcript excerpt, the specific lines Juno cited, not the whole interview, so the reviewer can check the claim against Sarah's actual words without leaving the canvas.
The decision field itself: three buttons, Approve, Edit, Reject, which is what writes the human-correction record from the expiry-and-write-rules section. Nothing on this canvas is labeled final until one of these is used.

Augmentation + Insights → the PM reviews a cited synthesis instead of assembling it from six documents themselves, and decides, rather than the model deciding for them.

The badge saves the work of remembering to check this signal against strategy, capacity, and roadmap. The canvas makes that synthesis inspectable, every claim cited, nothing paraphrased past what the roadmap actually says. The Approve/Edit/Reject field keeps the call with the PM, not Juno.

## Fallbacks

**Kill switch**

Level 1: Reject the draft (already built, in Pillar 3)

The Approve/Edit/Reject field on the Full-Page Canvas is the everyday override. A PM who thinks the P0 tier, the Platform & Infra assignment, or the roadmap disposition is wrong hits Reject, and per the expiry-and-write-rules section, that human correction permanently outranks Juno's stored draft. This handles "Juno's answer on this signal is wrong," the most common case.

Level 2: Stop the run, mid-process

A Stop control sits next to the "Juno is reviewing this signal…" badge from Pillar 1, live for as long as the breadcrumbs from Pillar 2 are ticking through. This covers a different case: the PM realizes partway through, maybe after seeing "Checking Platform & Infra capacity…", that this transcript shouldn't be triaged this way at all, or notices it's taking too long and they'd rather handle it manually. Hitting Stop halts the run immediately, same as hitting the turn/time/cost ceiling from the cost-and-latency section, and surfaces the same kind of partial-result label: "Stopped by [name] at read 3 of 6," not silently discarded.

Level 3: Disable the trigger

This is the actual kill switch, a toggle that turns off Pillar 1's trigger entirely, so new transcripts landing in Raw Signals no longer spawn a Juno run at all.

**Training signal**

Pillar 4: Captured Training Signal — What Gets Logged Back

Every override (Reject, Edit, Stop, trigger-disable) is logged as a labeled correction, not just an outcome, so it can be reviewed for patterns later. Nothing from it feeds back into Juno automatically.

That last part matters most: this PRD already ruled out cross-signal learning and any autonomous self-modification (deliberate-omissions section, memory section). So "captured training signal" here means data collected for a human to periodically review and use to improve the prompt, thresholds, or source list, not a live loop where Juno adjusts its own behavior based on past corrections.

What gets logged
Event	What's captured	Why it's useful
Reject (Level 1)	Which draft was rejected, which specific field was wrong (tier, owner, roadmap disposition, or confidence), and the reviewer's stated reason if they gave one	Tells you not just that Juno was wrong, but where. If "owner" is wrong repeatedly, that's a Capacity Note interpretation problem; if "tier" is wrong repeatedly, that's a Strategy framework problem.
Edit	A diff between Juno's original draft and the reviewer's edited version, field by field	Softer signal than Reject: Juno was mostly right but needed a correction. Useful for spotting a consistent bias (e.g., always slightly overconfident on corroboration).
Stop (Level 2)	Which read step was in progress when stopped, and the reviewer's reason if given	Flags cases where the pipeline itself is the problem, too slow, going somewhere the reviewer didn't want, rather than the output being wrong.
Every pre-ship gate result (from the verification section)	Which of the 8 checks passed or failed, per run, not just the final draft	This is what feeds the "3+ over-target runs in a week" review trigger from the cost-and-latency section, and separately shows which check fails most often.
Confidence score vs. reviewer outcome	Whether low-confidence drafts (below 60%) were rejected/edited more often than high-confidence ones	This is a calibration check: if high-confidence drafts get rejected just as often as low-confidence ones, the confidence score itself isn't measuring what it claims to.
Trigger-disable (Level 3)	When it happened, who did it, and any reason given	The strongest possible signal, it means overrides at Level 1 and 2 weren't enough and something structural needs revisiting.

**Fail-safe**

If this fails	Then Juno does this
Sarah's transcript is unreachable (the primary signal itself)	Surfaces "Cannot triage: primary signal unavailable" and produces no draft at all, no partial priority, no placeholder tier.
Strategy One-Pager missing or unreachable	Surfaces a "cautious mode" warning on the draft and drops priority confidence to N/A, it still shows the rest of the draft (owner, roadmap disposition) but will not assign a P0–P3 tier without the framework that defines what P0 means.
Slack thread (corroboration) missing or unreachable	Still produces a draft, but marks the failure as "reported by one user, unconfirmed" instead of "corroborated," and lowers the confidence score accordingly rather than treating silence as agreement.
Capacity Note missing or unreachable	The owner field reads "owner not verified" instead of naming Platform & Infra, and Juno does not recommend committing or deferring work, since it has no basis for a capacity claim.
Roadmap missing or unreachable	The disposition field reads "roadmap status unknown" instead of "committed" or "planned." Juno will not infer disposition from the Strategy doc or Capacity Note alone.
Pipeline Snapshot missing or unreachable	The revenue-impact line is omitted from the draft entirely, not estimated, not left as a stale figure from a prior run.
Two sources disagree on priority tier (the pattern we saw with the Pearson ticket, where Strategy calls it P0 and the roadmap treats it as P1)	Juno does not average, split the difference, or pick one silently. It flags the conflict explicitly, "Strategy and Roadmap disagree on priority," and routes to mandatory human review rather than resolving it.
The pre-ship gate itself errors out (a check fails to run, rather than a check catching a problem)	No draft is shown at all, this is treated as a system fault, not a content issue, logged and escalated to whoever maintains Juno, the same path as a Blocked-verb violation, since it means the safety mechanism itself broke.
