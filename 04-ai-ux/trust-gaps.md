## Trust gaps

| Gap | Where it shows up | User cost | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Black-box Gap** | Can the user see why the AI decided? | Noticeable but recoverable (4/5 closed) | Every claim in the draft carries a citation to one of the six named sources, shown inline on the Full-Page Canvas (transcript excerpt, Slack thread, Strategy pillar, Capacity Note line, roadmap wording, CRM entry). | **The Hallucination Gap** | Could this confidently be wrong? | Noticeable but recoverable (4/5 closed) | The pre-ship gate enforces zero tolerance on uncited claims, roadmap-wording drift, and excluded-topic leakage. Below 40% confidence, no tier is shown at all rather than a hedged guess. The fail-safe table means a missing or stale source is disclosed, never silently filled in. **Residual gap**: Layer 2's Accuracy and Confidence Calibration bars are deliberately set below 100% (≥90% and ≥85%), so some miscalibrated confidence is tolerated by design, and a single-source signal like Sarah's can still clear the confidence threshold on its own, which is exactly why the Ambiguity checkpoint exists as a human patch rather than an automated close. |
| **The Control Gap** | Can the user steer or stop? | Fully closed (5/5) | Juno holds no Write or Send permission at any tier, nothing it produces reaches a person or a system of record without a PM's Approve, Edit, or Reject. A PM can interrupt a run mid-process (Level 2 Stop) or disable the trigger entirely (Level 3 kill switch). A human correction permanently outranks any stored draft. No further mitigation identified as needed here. |
| **The Intelligence Tax** | Is the latency / privacy / cognitive load worth the value? | Noticeable but recoverable (3/5 closed) | Processing targets are set tight (under 15 seconds, under $0.05 per run), and the contextual badge keeps the at-a-glance cost low, a PM sees a priority chip and confidence dot before deciding whether to open the full canvas at all. |

## Highest-priority fix

**The Intelligence Tax** (3/5). This is the lowest-scored gap for two concrete reasons specific to this signal. First, the Full-Page Canvas for a red-zone item like Sarah's, one that's both a capacity risk and tied to Initech's revenue, is deliberately dense: six sources, a confidence panel, a freshness strip, and now a second reviewer (the Platform & Infra lead) pulled in. The tool's cognitive load goes up exactly for the cases where getting it wrong costs the most, which is the right tradeoff, but it means the tax is real, not eliminated. Second, there's no empirical evidence yet that Juno's draft actually beats manual synthesis time for a PM, the value proposition assumes this, but Layer 1's time-to-decision metric hasn't been measured against a real baseline at this harness's current volume (one signal). The fix isn't a redesign, it's closing this gap with data: track time-to-decision against a manually-synthesized baseline for the next several occurrences of this signal type, and only claim the tax is worth it once that's measured, not assumed.

## Verdict

**Hold.** Three of four gaps carry a documented residual risk (Black-box at 4/5, Hallucination at 4/5, Intelligence Tax at 3/5). Only the Control Gap is fully closed. Ship once the Intelligence Tax gap has real time-to-decision data behind it, or down-scope the red-zone canvas to reduce cognitive load until that data exists.
