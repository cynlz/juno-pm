# Agent Workflow Spec (AWSpec) · Juno

_Module 5. Generated by `M5 - Agent Workflow Spec Builder.html`. Paste over `05-agentic-workflows/awspec.md`._

## Goal

Reduce the risk of misprioritizing a reliability failure (Sarah's CSV export crash) by producing a cited, human-reviewed priority recommendation before an already fully-committed engineering pod is asked to act on it.

**Primary actor:** Agent + Human-in-the-loop

## Trigger

type == "user_interview_transcript" is what keeps this scoped to exactly the artifact type we selected. Without it, the trigger would also fire on Mike Ross's support ticket or Gavin's email, which we deliberately excluded back in the required-sources section. This one field is the entire boundary between "in scope" and "not."
no_existing_draft(artifact_id) is the dedupe check. Without it, the trigger would refire every time the same transcript is re-indexed or touched, producing duplicate drafts for the same signal. This is a testable, boolean condition, not a judgment call, so it belongs in the trigger itself rather than being handled downstream.
What's deliberately not in the trigger: content matching for "describes a failure." I considered adding a fourth condition like contains_keyword(["crash", "error", "broken"]) to only fire on transcripts that sound like a reliability complaint. I left it out because that's exactly the classification judgment we said Juno shouldn't make unsupervised (the router-decision section), and because it would silently narrow what counts as "worth triaging" without a human ever seeing the transcripts that got filtered out. Instead, a transcript that doesn't describe a real problem still gets triaged, and the cross-reference step in Pillar 2 is where that shows up, as a low-confidence, no-clear-tier draft, not as a silent non-trigger.

- **Semantic:** Out of scope: PM's preferred format, tone, or level of detail. We already established in the memory section that the draft isn't personalized per viewer, every PM reviewing the same signal sees the same draft. Building a "preferred risk format" preference would contradict that directly.
Out of scope: customer-specific facts (Initech's deal terms, expansion status, anything from the Pipeline Snapshot). These must always come fresh from the source, per the 24-hour freshness rule, not from a remembered summary. A deal that closed or stalled since the last run would go undetected if Juno relied on a memorized fact instead of rereading it.
Out of scope: "default owner" assumptions, e.g., hardcoding "Platform & Infra owns reporting issues" as a standing fact. That would bypass the Capacity Note's 7-day freshness check entirely, and the Capacity Note itself shows exactly why that's dangerous, it mentions a capacity fact the team "found out yesterday." A memorized default would go stale invisibly.
Out of scope: any adjustment learned from past Reject/Edit decisions. The captured-training-signal pillar was explicit that this feeds a periodic human review process, not live self-modification. If this were semantic memory the agent drew on automatically, it would be exactly the "quiet accumulation of behavior nobody signed off on" that the rest of this PRD has been designed to prevent.
Out of scope: reviewer identity or role mappings. Who's allowed to approve, edit, or flip the Level 3 kill switch is access-control configuration, not something the agent learns or remembers about people it's worked with.
- **Working:** In-scope, held live in the run's context window:

The current signal ID (Sarah's transcript's artifact ID), so every read, citation, and the eventual draft all anchor to the same signal, and the dedupe check (no_existing_draft(artifact_id)) has something to check against.
The retrieved content chunks from each of the 6 sources, as they're pulled in turn: the transcript itself, the Slack thread, the relevant Strategy One-Pager section, the Capacity Note's Platform & Infra paragraph, the two roadmap lines, and the Initech pipeline entry. These need to be live in context because the cross-reference step (Pillar 2) reasons across all of them at once, comparing what Strategy says against what Capacity and Roadmap say.
Per-source freshness tags, captured the moment each source is read, held live so the pre-ship gate can check "is this fresh enough" without re-fetching.
Running ceiling counters: turns used, elapsed time, cost accrued, checked against the 8-turn / 90-second / $0.50 ceilings after every step, not just at the end.
Intermediate flags computed during reasoning: whether the Slack thread corroborates the transcript, whether Strategy and Roadmap agree on tier, whether the confidence score is trending above or below 60%. These need to be live because later steps (drafting, gate-checking) branch on them.
Out of scope for working memory, retrieved instead, or not retrieved at all
Any other signal's draft or history. Even though the 90-day audit record exists, it should not be pulled into this run's live context. Retrieving Sarah's past drafts (if this signal recurred) would edge toward the cross-signal learning we ruled out in the semantic-memory answer; if it's ever needed, it should be a deliberate, visible lookup a human requests, not something silently loaded into the agent's working state.
The full Strategy One-Pager, Capacity Note, or Roadmap. Only the relevant sections need to be live (per the required-sources table from early in this PRD), the rest should stay retrievable on disk but not loaded into context, both for cost (per the latency-and-cost targets) and to avoid the model reasoning over content we deliberately excluded, like the Security & Compliance or App & Frontend paragraphs in the Capacity Note.
The other four Raw Signals artifacts (Pearson ticket, Gavin's email, Acme CRM notes, and the Slack thread treated as a standalone artifact rather than corroboration). These aren't retrieved into this run at all, live or otherwise, consistent with the router-decision section's scoping.
Reviewer identity and past review patterns. Retrievable elsewhere (for the training-signal review process), but not loaded into the agent's live context while it's producing this draft, since that's exactly the kind of profile-building the semantic-memory answer ruled out.
- **External:** Customer research repo API (read) — wherever Raw Signals actually lives (a research tool, shared drive, or intake system); supplies Sarah's transcript.
Slack API (read only) — the #backend-monitoring channel, for dev_dave's thread. No chat.postMessage scope granted at all, consistent with the Blocked tier on send_to_slack.
Docs/wiki API (read) — wherever the Strategy One-Pager and Engineering Capacity Note are maintained (Confluence, Notion, or similar); pulled per-section, not the full document, per the required-sources scoping.
Roadmap tool API (read only; write scope not granted) — supplies the two export-related lines. If the underlying tool (Productboard, Aha!, an internal roadmap doc) technically supports a write endpoint, Juno's credentials are provisioned read-only, so there's no write capability to accidentally call, not just a policy telling it not to.
CRM API (read only) — Salesforce or equivalent, supplies the Initech pipeline line from the Sales Ops snapshot.

## Human-in-the-loop

PM reviews every draft, standard priority, when confidence ≥ 60% and fewer than 2 sources are stale/unreachable. This is the default path: agent completes the full run, hands off once, PM sees a normal-labeled draft.
PM reviews with a mandatory-review flag when confidence < 60%. Same handoff point, but the draft is labeled "Needs review" with the specific reasons listed, rather than shown as standard.
PM reviews with a draft-only flag when 2 or more of the 6 sources are stale, cached, or unreachable. Control still passes back at the same point (end of run), but the draft can't be treated as a normal result.
PM's control interrupts the agent mid-run, at any point, via the Level 2 Stop control, regardless of confidence or tier. This is the one pass-back that isn't gated by a threshold at all, it's available the entire time the agent is running.
PM adjudicates directly, before a draft even finishes, if two sources disagree on priority tier (the Strategy-vs-Roadmap conflict pattern). Juno doesn't produce a resolved draft in this case; it halts and passes control back with the conflict named, not a recommendation.
Control passes to engineering, not the PM, on a system fault, specifically a Blocked-verb call or a pre-ship gate error. This is the one case where the human on the other end isn't the reviewer, it's whoever maintains Juno, because the failure is about the system doing something it shouldn't, not about the content of a draft.

## Success & failure

- **Done when:** Type	Precise trigger	What's handed back
Success	Draft completes, passes all 8 pre-ship gate checks, confidence ≥ 60%, fewer than 2 sources stale/unreachable	A standard-labeled draft: priority tier, owner, roadmap disposition, citations, confidence panel. Hands to the PM at the Approve/Edit/Reject decision field.
Failure	The primary signal (Sarah's transcript) can't be read, and the single retry also fails	No draft at all. Hands back immediately with an explicit notice: "Cannot triage: primary signal unavailable." Nothing partial is shown, since there's nothing to triage without the signal itself.
Escalation (to the PM)	Two or more of the 6 sources are stale, cached, or unreachable, OR two sources disagree on priority tier (the Strategy-vs-Roadmap conflict pattern)	A draft that's explicitly flagged, either "draft-only, not final" with the affected sources named, or, in the conflict case, no resolved tier at all, just the conflict stated for the PM to adjudicate.
Escalation (to engineering)	A Blocked-verb call is attempted, or the pre-ship gate itself errors out rather than catching something	The run halts entirely. No draft goes to the PM. This is logged and routed to whoever maintains Juno, since it signals a system fault, not a content problem.
Timeout	8 turns used, or 90 seconds elapsed, or $0.50 in cost consumed, whichever comes first	A partial result labeled incomplete, showing exactly which of the six sources were read before the ceiling hit, so the PM can finish manually or trigger a rerun.
Manual interrupt	The PM hits the Level 2 Stop control mid-run	Whatever was completed up to that point, labeled "Stopped by [name] at read N of 6," never silently discarded.
