## What graders score

- **Task / product:** Juno, RocketShip's internal AI triage copilot. Use case: Signal triage, turning one raw customer signal (Sarah's user interview transcript describing a CSV export crash) into a cited, human-reviewed recommendation covering priority tier, owning pod, and roadmap disposition.
- **Reviewer audience:** The engineers who built Juno. They can verify the system behaved as designed (did it stay within its tool boundaries, did the gate checks run correctly), but they shouldn't be the ones judging whether the triage call itself was good, that's a product judgment, not an engineering one, and self-grading the thing you built is a poor substitute for the PM's independent read. Sarah, or any customer. They have no visibility into RocketShip's internal capacity constraints or roadmap tradeoffs, which is most of what this rubric needs to assess. A general crowd-sourced or third-party rater pool. This use case depends on RocketShip-specific context (the Strategy One-Pager's framework, the Capacity Note's pod allocations) that only an internal PM realistically holds. A rater without that context could only judge surface fluency, not correctness.
- **Value proposition:** Accuracy: the priority tier, owner, and roadmap disposition actually match what the six named sources support, not what sounds plausible.
Traceability: every claim in the draft is cited to a real source, per the pre-ship gate's zero-tolerance rule. A PM shouldn't have to go re-check Juno's homework to trust a citation is real.
Calibrated uncertainty: the confidence score reflects genuine uncertainty (like Sarah never stating a file size) rather than either false confidence or reflexive hedging. A PM should be able to tell, from the score alone, how much scrutiny a draft needs.
Safe failure: when a source is missing, stale, or in conflict with another, Juno says so rather than guessing, per the fail-safe table. This is arguably the highest bar, since a single confidently-wrong draft costs more trust than several honestly-uncertain ones.
Time saved: the entire point of the augmentation framing from Pillar 3, a PM should reach a decision faster with Juno's draft than they would cross-referencing Strategy, Capacity, Roadmap, and Pipeline by hand.

## Calibration

- **Sampling rule:** 100% of red-zone drafts, 100% of Reject/Edit outcomes, and a 20% random sample of everything else that clears review without incident.
- **Cadence:** Cadence	What happens	Why this timing Weekly	The PM reviewer audience scores that week's sampled drafts (all red-zone and Reject/Edit cases, plus the 20% random sample) against the five dimensions	Matches the review trigger already set in the cost-and-latency section (3+ over-target runs in a week prompts investigation). Keeping rubric review on the same weekly rhythm means quality and performance get looked at together, not on separate clocks. Quarterly	A full rubric recalibration: do the five dimensions still make sense, are the sampling percentages catching real issues, does "Accuracy" still mean the same thing	Aligned to the same quarter turnover that re-pins the Strategy One-Pager's P0–P3 framework (next: Oct 1). Since Accuracy is graded against that framework, the rubric itself needs re-validating whenever the framework it's measuring against changes. Immediately, out of cadence	An unscheduled rubric review, triggered the moment the Level 3 kill switch (disabling Juno's trigger entirely) is used	This is already the strongest signal in the whole system that something structural broke, per the Feedback Loop pillar. Waiting for the next weekly cycle to review it would be a poor match for how serious that event is.
- **Graders per item:** Item type	Graders	Why Routine sampled draft (the 20% random sample)	1 — the reviewing PM	This is a calibration spot-check, not an audit. A single grader is enough to catch drift over time across many samples; doubling every routine item wouldn't meaningfully improve signal for the added cost. Reject / Edit outcome	2 — the reviewing PM plus one additional PM	These are the highest-signal disagreement cases. A second, independent grader helps tell apart "Juno actually got this wrong" from "this PM's judgment call is unusual," which a single grader's score can't distinguish on its own. Red-zone draft (P0 + no-slack owner, ≥$100k account, single-source ambiguity)	2, and one must be the specific consulted reviewer named in the Checkpoints contract	For a capacity-risk red zone, that's the Platform & Infra lead; for a value-risk red zone, that's the account-owning stakeholder. This matches who the Checkpoints contract already says gets pulled in, the rubric scoring reuses that same person rather than inventing a separate evaluator.
- **Calibration cadence:** Element	Detail Frequency	Monthly, sitting between the weekly scoring cadence and the quarterly full rubric recalibration already established What's scored	A fixed gold set, not that month's live drafts, so every grader is judging identical material and any score difference reflects grader disagreement, not different drafts Who participates	Every PM in the reviewer pool, plus the Platform & Infra lead when a capacity-related item is in the gold set What happens on disagreement	The group discusses the specific dimension where scores diverged and agrees on what the band boundary actually means going forward; this feeds directly into the quarterly rubric recalibration if the disagreement points to a genuinely ambiguous dimension definition, not just an inconsistent grader

The two PMs don't settle it between themselves. Neither talks the other into their score. Two people converging on a compromise isn't independent judgment, it's just whoever argues more persuasively, which defeats the reason a second grader scored it at all.
It escalates to a fixed third grader, the PM who owns Juno overall, not whichever colleague is nearby. Their score on Confidence Calibration for this draft is final.
Because this signal ties to Initech's $120k expansion, per the Checkpoints contract this item already sits in the value red-zone. That means the escalation happens immediately, not batched into the next weekly or monthly cycle, the same urgency the Checkpoints contract already assigned to this exact account.
The disagreement is logged in full: both original scores, the dimension (Confidence Calibration), the specific reasoning each grader gave (one citing the missing file size, the other citing the inferential corroboration), and the third grader's final call. Nothing about this gets quietly resolved and forgotten, consistent with the never-silently-drop principle running through this entire PRD.
If Confidence Calibration keeps triggering disagreement across other signals later (once Juno handles more than this one), that's raised at the next monthly calibration session as evidence the dimension's definition is ambiguous, not that either PM graded badly.

## Pass bar

Citation & Traceability — one uncited or fabricated claim, anywhere in the draft, fails it outright. Failure Handling — if a source was missing, stale, or conflicting and the draft didn't say so plainly (say, it treated a stale Capacity Note as current, or guessed at Sarah's file size instead of flagging the gap), that's an automatic fail, regardless of how the rest of the draft reads. Scope Discipline — any reference to an excluded artifact (Pearson's ticket, Gavin's email, Acme's SSO or TikTok notes) fails the draft, even in passing.
